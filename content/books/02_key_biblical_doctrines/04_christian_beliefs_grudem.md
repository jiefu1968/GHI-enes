# Christian Beliefs: Twenty Basics for New Believers (based on Wayne Grudem's condensed framework)

## Overview

This is the most condensed and accessible of the four frameworks in
this module, distilling systematic theology into twenty short units
aimed specifically at new believers rather than trained leaders. Each
unit ends with review and application questions, making it especially
well suited as a template for discipleship curricula for recent
converts in oral or low-literacy contexts, where twenty short, memorable
units are far more reproducible than one long comprehensive text.

## The Bible: Authority, Clarity, Necessity, Sufficiency

The opening unit establishes four properties of Scripture that recur
throughout the rest of the material: its authority (it carries God's
own authority, not merely human wisdom), its clarity (its central
message can be understood by ordinary readers without specialized
training), its necessity (saving knowledge of God requires it — general
revelation alone is not enough), and its sufficiency (it contains
everything needed for faith and life, without needing supplementation
from other authorities). For missionaries training new leaders who may
never attend seminary, these four properties function as a reassurance:
ordinary believers, without formal theological education, are fully
capable of understanding and teaching Scripture's central message.

## The Character of God

An unusually long list of divine attributes is presented as short,
digestible units, each answering "God is ___": existing, knowable,
independent, unchangeable, eternal, omnipresent, spirit, invisible,
omniscient, wise, truthful, good, loving, holy, righteous and just,
jealous (in the sense of rightly desiring exclusive worship), wrathful
toward sin, sovereign over his own will, free, omnipotent, perfect,
blessed, beautiful, and one in essence though three in person. Breaking
God's character into short standalone units like this is particularly
useful for oral learners and new believers, since each attribute can be
taught, discussed, and reviewed as a self-contained lesson rather than
requiring the whole doctrine of God to be grasped at once.

## The Trinity, Creation, and Humanity

Later units address the Trinity (one God in three persons), creation
(the world's origin and God's ongoing relationship to it), and the
nature of humanity (created in God's image, distinguishing male and
female, and the reality and consequences of sin). Presenting these as
separate short units, each with its own review questions, again
supports a training model where an indigenous leader can teach one
unit per gathering without needing the full theological apparatus a
seminary-trained pastor would have.

## Application to missionary training

The twenty-unit structure itself is a transferable discipleship model:
rather than only using this content as background reading for the
missionary, it can be adapted directly as a template for a 20-session
new-believer class, translated and taught unit by unit in the target
language, with the built-in review questions providing a ready-made
comprehension check at each stage. This addresses a common gap in
church planting — having a scalable, reproducible way to establish
basic doctrinal literacy in brand-new believers without requiring years
of formal training first.

## Discussion prompts for training

- Which of the twenty attributes of God would be hardest to explain to
  someone from your target culture, and why?
- How might you adapt this twenty-unit structure into a discipleship
  track for new believers in an oral culture with no access to written
  materials?
- Why does teaching Scripture's "sufficiency" early matter for how a
  new leader will handle later questions or challenges to the faith?
