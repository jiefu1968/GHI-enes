# The Character of a Follower of Jesus: Growing in Christlike Character (based on a Navigators discipleship framework)

## Overview

This framework is a practical, workbook-style approach to Christian
character formation, organized around specific virtues a believer is
meant to grow into rather than around abstract doctrine. Its method
combines short teaching on a virtue with guided Bible study, Scripture
memorization, and personal journaling — reflecting a broader
discipleship tradition (associated with the Navigators movement) that
emphasizes personal spiritual multiplication: every believer trained
well enough to train the next.

## Growing in the Fruit of the Spirit

The starting point is Galatians 5's picture of Spirit-produced character
(love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness,
gentleness, self-control) as the natural outcome of abiding in Christ,
not a checklist achieved through willpower. The instruction to
"accept by faith" the victory already won, rather than white-knuckling
moral effort, sets the tone for the rest of the material: character
change is framed as cooperating with the Spirit's work, not
self-generated moral improvement.

## Growing in Holiness and Personal Purity

A significant portion addresses sexual purity and holy living directly
and practically — treating this not as a source of shame to avoid
discussing, but as an area requiring specific, concrete strategies
(accountability relationships, prayer, honest self-examination) rather
than willpower alone. Marriage is addressed as a distinct topic within
this section, framing sexual faithfulness within marriage as one
expression of a broader pattern of holy living rather than an isolated
rule.

## Growing in Unselfish Love

Love is broken down into specific, observable expressions rather than
treated as a vague feeling: humility (love expressed through how one
regards others), speech (love expressed through what one says and how),
and good works (love expressed through concrete action). This
breakdown gives missionary trainers a practical rubric for evaluating
whether "love" is actually being lived out in a specific relationship,
rather than remaining an abstract ideal.

## Growing in Integrity and Honesty

A further section addresses the ongoing struggle for integrity,
including honesty in speech specifically — treating small,
everyday untruths (exaggeration, convenient omission, flattery) as
worth addressing directly rather than only large, dramatic lies. This
granular approach to honesty is particularly relevant cross-culturally,
since norms around social truth-telling, flattery, and "saving face"
vary significantly between cultures, and a missionary's own example of
integrity in small matters is often what indigenous believers observe
and imitate most closely.

## Method: Study, Memorization, Journaling

Each unit follows a consistent pattern: a short teaching section, a
guided study of relevant biblical passages, a suggested verse for
memorization, a personal journal prompt, and a summary of key points to
remember. This method is itself transferable: it models a simple,
repeatable discipleship format (teach a virtue, study Scripture on it,
memorize a verse, reflect personally) that a missionary can hand off to
a new believer or indigenous leader to use with others, rather than
requiring an outside teacher indefinitely.

## Application to cross-cultural and diaspora ministry

Because character formation here is tied to specific, memorizable
Scripture and a repeatable personal study method, it travels well into
contexts with limited access to trained teachers. The virtue-by-virtue
structure (purity, love, honesty, fruit of the Spirit) also gives
missionaries a diagnostic tool: when discipling someone long-term, this
framework offers a checklist of character areas to address explicitly,
rather than assuming spiritual growth will address every area
automatically.

## Discussion prompts for training

- Which specific fruit of the Spirit is currently hardest for you to
  "accept by faith" rather than try to produce through willpower alone?
- How does breaking "love" into humility, speech, and good works help
  you evaluate a specific relationship in your own ministry?
- What small, everyday form of dishonesty (exaggeration, flattery,
  convenient omission) is most normalized in your target culture, and
  how would you address it without shaming the listener?
