# Disciple Making Movements (DMM): Obedience-Based Church Planting

## Overview

This framework presents a Disciple Making Movements (DMM) approach to
church planting, built around a simple, consistently repeated
conviction: authentic church planting begins with making obedient
disciples, not with building institutional church structures first.
The training emphasizes listening directly to God, radical simplicity,
and reproducibility as the conditions under which genuine
multiplication movements emerge, rather than depending on a trained
missionary's continued presence or elaborate programming.

## Foundational Commitments

The approach opens with five core commitments: laying aside
unnecessary inherited church traditions that hinder reproducibility,
prioritizing sustained prayer, actively engaging among the spiritually
lost rather than waiting for them to come to church, deliberately
training and mentoring others rather than doing all the ministry work
personally, and maintaining ongoing relational connection with those
being discipled. Genesis of the approach is grounded in Jesus' own
teaching that people who come to him are those the Father himself has
already been teaching (John 6:43-45) — reframing the missionary's role
as helping people recognize and respond to what God is already doing,
rather than being the sole source of spiritual insight.

## Love, Obedience, and the Shema Lifestyle

Following the Shema's pattern (Deuteronomy 6) of hearing and obeying
together as a single, inseparable act, the framework insists genuine
love for God is demonstrated through obedience (John 14:15) lived out
daily rather than through correct belief alone. This produces a
"Shema lifestyle" — naturally speaking about God in ordinary
conversation and demonstrating faith through consistent action, making
faith an integrated, daily reality rather than a separate religious
compartment of life.

## Being Like Jesus: Incarnational Presence

Disciples are trained to adopt Christ's own humble, self-emptying
attitude (Philippians 2) and sent posture (John 20), actively
identifying and removing unhelpful cultural baggage that has
historically accompanied Christian witness in many contexts, adapting
genuinely to the local culture without compromising biblical truth.

## Teaching Obedience, Not Just Information

Central to the Great Commission (Matthew 28) is teaching new believers
to obey Christ's commands, not merely transmitting doctrinal
information — a distinction the framework treats as decisive for
whether genuine transformation and multiplication actually occurs.
Discipleship is intentionally directed at groups rather than isolated
individuals, and discipling relationships often begin before formal
baptism, integrating new believers into obedient practice immediately
rather than delaying real discipleship until after a formal
membership step.

## Finding the Person of Peace

Drawing on Jesus' own sending strategy, disciples actively seek a
"Person of Peace" — someone open, welcoming, willing to share with
others, and influential within their existing social network — as a
strategic entry point into a whole network of relationships rather than
evangelizing randomly and individually. Practical tools for
identifying a Person of Peace include living the Shema lifestyle
openly, sharing personal testimony, asking spiritual questions,
praying for people's felt needs, and — in relevant cultural contexts —
using shared sacred texts (such as the Quran) as a respectful bridge
into conversation about Christ. The guiding principle is filtering for
receptivity rather than trying to convince or argue everyone into
faith.

## Reaching Groups Through Oikos and Discovery Groups

Drawing on the pattern in Acts, where conversion frequently happened at
the level of whole households and social networks (oikos) rather than
isolated individuals, the strategy deliberately targets families and
existing social groups rather than only individual converts.
Discovery Groups use a deliberately non-lecturing method: facilitators
ask questions and let Scripture itself teach, rather than delivering
content through traditional preaching or instruction, following a
repeatable seven-question pattern moving from thankfulness and
struggle through reading, retelling, and reflecting on a Bible passage
to concrete personal obedience.

## The Full Church Planting Process and Core Principles

The complete process moves through prayer, gaining relational access,
finding a Person of Peace, focusing on their existing network,
starting a Bible discovery group, raising up local leadership from
within that group, multiplying additional discovery groups, and
ultimately forming reproducing house churches. Underlying principles
recur throughout: obedience matters more than mere knowledge,
relationship matters more than programs, simplicity is what enables
multiplication (complexity kills reproducibility), local leadership
development is essential from the earliest stages, and the entire
process depends on Spirit-led learning rather than solely missionary-
delivered instruction.

## Application to cross-cultural and missionary training

This framework's central principle — build disciples first and let
the church emerge from genuinely obedient, multiplying disciples,
rather than building institutional church structures first and hoping
discipleship follows — offers missionaries a concrete, reproducible
alternative to resource-intensive church-starting models. Its emphasis
on simplicity and reproducibility directly addresses the multiplication
problem many conventional approaches struggle with: a process only a
trained specialist can lead will never multiply beyond that
specialist's own capacity, while a genuinely simple, Spirit-led,
question-based process ordinary new believers can immediately
reproduce with others is what actually produces multiplying movements.

## Discussion prompts for training

- How would you find and correctly discern a genuine "Person of
  Peace" in your specific ministry network, rather than mistaking
  mere friendliness for real spiritual openness?
- Why does this framework insist discipleship should often begin
  before baptism rather than after it, and what would that look like
  practically in your context?
- What about your current ministry approach might be too complex for
  an ordinary new believer to immediately reproduce with someone else,
  and how could you simplify it?
