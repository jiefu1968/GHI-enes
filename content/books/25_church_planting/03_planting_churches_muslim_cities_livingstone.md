# Planting Churches in Muslim Cities (based on Greg Livingstone's framework)

## Overview

Written by the founder of the Frontiers mission agency after decades
of direct experience and years of research, this framework addresses
the specific strategic challenge of establishing genuine, rooted
churches among unreached Muslim urban populations. Its central,
sobering observation is that although Christianity claims the nominal
allegiance of a large share of the world's population, the church has
in fact become nearly extinct, deeply hidden, or never successfully
rooted among large segments of the world's Muslim population — making
strategic, sustained church planting specifically targeting Muslim
cities an urgent unfinished task rather than a completed one.

## The Scope of the Challenge

Major cities with significant Muslim populations represent some of the
least-reached urban environments in the world for genuine, visible
Christian community, despite centuries of historical Christian
presence in the wider region. This framework treats each such city as
requiring its own dedicated strategic attention — mapping specific
barriers, receptive networks, and access points — rather than applying
a single generic global strategy uniformly across radically different
Muslim urban contexts.

## Strategic and Practical Considerations

Church planting among Muslim populations requires sustained attention
to practical access (given legal and social restrictions common in
many Muslim-majority contexts), culturally appropriate approaches to
evangelism and discipleship that take Islamic worldview and social
structure seriously, and patient, long-term relational investment
rather than short-term campaign-style evangelistic strategy. The
framework draws on extensive practitioner experience and doctoral-level
research to propose specific, field-tested strategic principles for
sustained urban church planting in these particularly challenging
contexts.

## Application to cross-cultural and missionary training

This framework directly equips missionaries specifically targeting
unreached Muslim urban populations with strategic categories developed
from decades of dedicated organizational experience (through Frontiers
and similar agencies) rather than generic global church-planting
principles applied without adaptation to the specific legal, social,
and worldview challenges of Muslim urban contexts.

## Discussion prompts for training

- What specific legal, social, or worldview barriers does your
  particular Muslim urban context present that a generic global
  church-planting strategy might not adequately address?
- How would you balance patient, long-term relational investment
  against the urgency this framework expresses about currently
  unreached Muslim populations?
- What does genuine, rooted (rather than merely nominal or hidden)
  Christian community look like in a specific Muslim city you are
  familiar with or serving in?
