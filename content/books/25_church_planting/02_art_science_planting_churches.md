# The Art and Science of Planting Churches: Rediscovering First-Century Ministry

## Overview

This framework distinguishes genuine "church planting" — organic,
first-century-style multiplication of disciples into reproducing
communities — from what it terms mere "church starting," a
contemporary pattern that treats launching a new congregation as
primarily a logistical and marketing exercise rather than a
disciple-making movement. Drawing on hard historical and
contemporary data about church decline and closure, the framework
argues for recovering biblical patterns of planting rather than
importing corporate-style church-launch methodology.

## Church Plantology: Starting Versus Planting

A foundational distinction separates "church starting" (raising
significant funds, recruiting a critical mass of people, designing
appealing branding, renting a building, and promoting heavily to fill
seats on a launch day) from genuine "church planting" (organic
disciple-making multiplication modeled on first-century practice).
The framework treats this distinction as more than semantic — church
starting, however successful by attendance metrics, often fails to
produce the reproducing, self-multiplying disciple-making culture that
defines authentic New Testament church planting.

## The Sobering Data on Church Decline

Contemporary church statistics (particularly in the North American
context) show a long, steady decline: church-to-population ratios have
fallen dramatically from twenty-eight churches per ten thousand
Americans in 1900 to roughly eleven per ten thousand by the early
2010s, alongside significant annual church closures and declining
religious affiliation more broadly. This data functions as the
framework's motivating urgency: conventional, program-heavy
church-starting approaches have not reversed this decline, suggesting
a genuine strategic rethink — returning to first-century patterns of
planting — is warranted rather than optional.

## The Planter's Rookie Mistakes

Common early mistakes church planters make include over-reliance on
attractive programming and event-based launch strategies rather than
organic relationship and disciple-making, underestimating the
difficulty and duration of the planting process, and mistaking initial
attendance growth for genuine spiritual depth and reproducing
disciple-making capacity within the new congregation.

## Biblical Patterns of Planting

The framework examines the New Testament's own church-planting pattern
directly — drawing especially on the apostle Paul's method (in
conversation with classic treatments such as Roland Allen's) and the
broader missionary practice recorded in Acts — to identify
transferable, reproducible principles rather than culturally specific,
first-century-only practices. This includes attention to how quickly
the earliest churches multiplied and how simply structured their early
practice remained, in deliberate contrast to the resource-intensive,
professionally staffed model many contemporary church starts assume is
necessary.

## Application to cross-cultural and missionary training

This framework's core distinction — church starting versus authentic
church planting — offers missionaries a valuable diagnostic question
for evaluating their own strategy: does a specific planting effort
depend on continued external funding, professional staffing, and
attractive programming to survive, or does it produce a genuinely
reproducing disciple-making culture capable of multiplying without
those resources? This question matters especially for missionaries
working in resource-limited or access-restricted contexts, where a
resource-intensive "church starting" model is often simply
impossible, making the recovery of simpler, first-century-style
church planting patterns not merely preferable but strategically
necessary.

## Discussion prompts for training

- How would you honestly evaluate whether your own church-planting
  strategy is closer to "church starting" (funding, branding,
  programming) or authentic "church planting" (organic disciple-making
  multiplication)?
- What specific "rookie mistakes" from this framework have you
  observed or made yourself in a planting effort?
- How does the sobering data on long-term church decline change how
  you evaluate a planting strategy's short-term attendance success?
