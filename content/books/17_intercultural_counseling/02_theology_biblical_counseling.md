# A Theology of Biblical Counseling: Scripture, Common Grace, and the Person of the Counselor

## Overview

This framework works through foundational theological questions any
serious counseling approach must answer: what role Scripture plays in
counseling, how common grace (God's general, non-saving kindness
extended to all people) relates to insights from secular psychology,
and how a counselor's own theology of God and humanity shapes the
counseling relationship itself, often illustrated through real case
narrative reflection rather than abstract principle alone.

## Counseling and Theology: Why the Connection Is Crucial

Every counseling approach, whether explicitly religious or not, rests
on unstated theological or philosophical assumptions about human
nature, the source of psychological distress, and what genuine healing
looks like. Treating these assumptions as neutral or purely
"scientific" obscures the real theological commitments — about
whether humans are fundamentally good, morally responsible, spiritual
beings, or purely biochemical systems — that shape every counseling
model's practical recommendations, whether the practitioner
acknowledges this or not.

## A Theology of Scripture in Counseling

Scripture's sufficiency for counseling is examined carefully — not as
a claim that the Bible addresses every clinical or practical
particular a counselor might encounter, but that Scripture provides
the necessary categories (sin, suffering, identity, hope, redemption)
within which any specific practical wisdom must be rightly understood
and applied. This avoids two opposite errors: treating Scripture as a
comprehensive clinical manual requiring no other practical knowledge,
or treating it as merely inspirational background irrelevant to actual
counseling practice.

## A Theology of Common Grace

Common grace — God's non-saving kindness and wisdom made available
generally to all humanity, believers and unbelievers alike — provides
a theological framework for evaluating insights from secular
psychology and counseling research without either uncritically
absorbing them or dismissing them wholesale as inherently corrupt.
Genuine, observable truths about human psychology, relationship
patterns, and healing processes discovered through general (non-
biblical) research can reflect God's common grace at work in creation,
even when the researchers themselves operate from a naturalistic
framework — requiring careful, theologically discerning evaluation
rather than uncritical adoption or blanket rejection.

## The Counselor as a Person, Not Just a Technician

A recurring theme is that a counselor's own character, theology, and
walk with God shape the counseling relationship as much as any
specific technique they employ — counseling is treated as a
fundamentally relational, pastoral encounter rather than a purely
technical service delivered by an interchangeable expert. Real case
reflection illustrates how a counselor's own theological assumptions
(about change, human agency, and grace) inevitably surface in how they
guide a counselee through difficulty, whether the counselor
consciously intends this or not.

## Application to cross-cultural and missionary training

This framework equips missionaries to evaluate secular counseling
techniques and locally available psychological resources with
theological discernment rather than uncritical adoption or blanket
rejection — recognizing genuine common-grace insight where it exists
while filtering every technique through Scripture's own categories.
Its emphasis on the counselor's own character and theology as
inseparable from technique also matters directly for training
indigenous counselors: theological formation, not merely technique
transfer, is essential preparation for counseling ministry.

## Discussion prompts for training

- Where have you seen genuine insight from secular psychology that
  reflects God's common grace, and how would you evaluate it
  theologically rather than adopting or rejecting it uncritically?
- How does treating Scripture as sufficient for the necessary
  categories of counseling (rather than a comprehensive clinical
  manual) change how you would use both Scripture and other practical
  resources together?
- In what way does your own theology of human nature and change
  currently shape your counseling practice, whether you have
  consciously examined this or not?
