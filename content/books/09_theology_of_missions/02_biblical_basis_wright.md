# The Biblical Basis for Mission Across the Whole Canon

A common mistake is to treat "mission" as a New Testament add-on, mainly the Great Commission (Matt 28:18-20) plus Acts. Christopher Wright's influential reframing ("The Mission of God") argues that mission is the interpretive key to the *entire* biblical story — not a theme found in a handful of proof-texts, but the trajectory the whole narrative is moving toward.

## Key waypoints
- **Genesis 12:1-3** — the Abrahamic call is explicitly universal in its intent: God blesses Abraham *so that* "all peoples on earth will be blessed through you." Israel's election was never an end in itself; it was instrumental to a purpose for all nations.
- **The Exodus** — God's rescue of Israel is simultaneously a self-revelation to the nations ("that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth," Exod 9:16); Israel's story is never only about Israel.
- **The Law and the nations** — provisions for the "sojourner" (foreigner) within Israel and Israel's calling to be "a kingdom of priests" (Exod 19:6) point outward, not merely inward.
- **The Prophets** — a recurring vision of the nations streaming to Zion to worship the God of Israel (Isaiah 2:2-4, 60; Micah 4:1-3) shows mission was always understood eschatologically, as a future hope, not only a present command.
- **The Gospels** — Jesus' ministry has both a centripetal dimension (people come to him) and increasingly, especially post-resurrection, a centrifugal command: "go" (Matt 28:19), "you will be my witnesses" (Acts 1:8).
- **The Great Commission and its parallels** — Matt 28:18-20 (make disciples of all nations), Luke 24:47 (repentance and forgiveness proclaimed to all nations), John 20:21 (sent as the Father sent the Son), Acts 1:8 (Spirit-empowered witness to the ends of the earth) — four distinct wordings of the same sending mandate, each emphasizing a different facet (authority, content, model, power).
- **Revelation 7:9** — the consummation: a multitude "from every nation, tribe, people and language" worshipping before the throne. This is the terminus the whole story has been moving toward, and it functions as the theological warrant for prioritizing unreached peoples (see Module 10, Major Mission Strategies).

## The interpretive payoff
Reading the whole canon this way changes how a missionary reads Scripture devotionally and teaches it cross-culturally: rather than searching for isolated "mission verses," every book of the Bible can be asked, "How does this passage advance or illuminate God's purpose to bless all nations?" This is also a corrective to Misreading the Scripture (Module 7) — an ethnocentric reading treats the Bible as a book about "us"; a missional reading recovers its outward orientation from the opening chapters of Genesis onward.
