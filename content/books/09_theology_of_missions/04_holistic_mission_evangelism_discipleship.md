# Holistic Mission, and Distinguishing Evangelism, Discipleship, and Mission

Two closely related but distinct conversations sit at the center of contemporary mission theology: (1) how proclamation and social action relate to each other, and (2) how the terms "evangelism," "discipleship," and "mission" relate to each other. Confusing these terms is one of the most common sources of muddled strategy on a ministry team.

## Holistic mission: the evangelism/social-action debate
Through the mid-20th century, mission practice sometimes split into two camps: one prioritizing verbal gospel proclamation and conversion almost exclusively, the other emphasizing social/structural transformation (poverty, justice, development) sometimes with proclamation minimized or absent. The **Lausanne Congress of 1974** and its Covenant became a widely cited turning point for evangelicals, affirming that evangelism and social responsibility are both part of Christian duty, without collapsing one into the other or making either merely instrumental to the other (i.e., social action is not just "bait" for evangelism, and evangelism is not dispensable in favor of social action).

Subsequent Lausanne-related documents and debates (Lausanne II in Manila, Cape Town 2010, and ongoing "holistic mission" or "integral mission" writing) have continued to refine exactly how proclamation and social action relate — whether social action is a *result* of the gospel, a *partner* alongside it, or a *component* of gospel proclamation itself. This remains a live and legitimately debated question rather than a fully settled one; different traditions within evangelicalism weight it somewhat differently.

## Evangelism, discipleship, and mission are not synonyms
It is common, especially in casual usage, to use "missions" to mean "evangelism," or to treat "discipleship" and "evangelism" interchangeably. A clearer set of distinctions:

- **Evangelism** — the initial proclamation of the gospel with a view to repentance and faith; a specific *act* or *event* (though it may take place over an extended relationship).
- **Discipleship** — the ongoing, lifelong process of formation into Christlikeness following conversion (see Module 8, Intercultural Discipleship); a *process*, not a single event.
- **Mission** — the church's entire sent purpose in the world, of which evangelism and discipleship are two essential components alongside others (church planting, mercy ministry, cultural engagement, prayer, worship among the nations). Mission is the *umbrella category*; evangelism and discipleship are stages or dimensions within it, not the whole of it.

## Practical payoff
A team that only evangelizes without discipling produces converts without maturing disciples or reproducing churches (see the Four-Self principles in Module 13). A team that only "does discipleship" within an already-Christian population while never crossing into evangelistic proclamation among the unreached has substituted pastoral care for mission. Naming which of the three — evangelism, discipleship, or the fuller category of mission — a given activity actually is keeps a team's strategy and its stated goals aligned.
