# Mary, the Saints, Purgatory, and Prayer for the Dead

## Mary
Catholic Marian doctrine includes four major dogmas: **Theotokos** ("God-bearer" / Mother of God — a title actually affirmed at the Council of Ephesus in 431 AD and held by many Protestants too, since it protects the doctrine of Christ's full deity rather than elevating Mary per se), **Perpetual Virginity** (that Mary remained a virgin throughout her life), the **Immaculate Conception** (that Mary herself was conceived without original sin, a 19th-century dogma — not to be confused with the virgin birth of Jesus, a very common point of confusion), and the **Assumption** (that Mary was taken up bodily into heaven at the end of her life, a 20th-century dogma).

Evangelicals honor Mary as the mother of Jesus and hold her up as an example of faith and obedience (her "let it be to me according to your word," Luke 1:38, is frequently cited as a model response to God's call) but do not generally direct prayer to her and do not accept the later Marian dogmas (Immaculate Conception, Assumption) as biblically required beliefs.

**Conversational note**: it is worth distinguishing carefully — Theotokos is actually a point of possible common ground (it's fundamentally a statement about Christ, not a Marian elevation), while the later dogmas (Immaculate Conception, Assumption) are the more substantive points of disagreement, since they lack the same explicit early conciliar and biblical grounding.

## The saints
Catholic teaching holds that departed saints may be asked to intercede — to pray on behalf of the person asking, similar to asking a living Christian friend to pray for you, not worship directed at the saint (worship, in Catholic teaching, belongs to God alone; this distinction between *latria*, worship due to God alone, and *dulia*, honor given to saints, is important not to flatten). Evangelicals hold that Christ alone is the mediator between God and humanity (1 Tim 2:5) and that prayer should be directed to God through Christ alone, without appeal to departed saints as intermediaries.

## Purgatory
Catholic teaching describes purgatory as a temporary state of purification for those who die in God's grace (already destined for heaven) but are not yet fully purified of the effects of sin. Commonly cited support includes 2 Maccabees 12:38-46 (from the Apocrypha, not part of the Protestant canon) and 1 Corinthians 3:10-15 (Paul's image of a person's work being tested by fire, with the person themselves "saved, but only as through fire").

Evangelicals reject purgatory, holding that Christ's atonement is fully sufficient and complete, and that a believer enters directly into Christ's presence at death without any further purification process. Commonly cited support includes Hebrews 9:27 (it is appointed for man to die once, then judgment — no intermediate purifying stage described), Luke 23:43 (Jesus's promise to the repentant thief, "today you will be with me in paradise" — immediate, not delayed, entry), and 2 Corinthians 5:8 ("to be absent from the body" is "to be present with the Lord" — immediate presence, not a purifying interval).

## Candles and prayer for the dead
Lighting candles in Catholic practice expresses prayer, hope, and remembrance for the deceased — the candle itself is understood to carry no saving power in official teaching, though it can be experienced very differently in popular piety. Evangelicals generally do not practice prayer for the dead or candle-lighting for that purpose, emphasizing instead the comfort of the living and the proclamation of the hope of the resurrection as the appropriate response to death and grief.

## Summary table of core differences
| Topic | Catholic | Evangelical |
|---|---|---|
| Mary | Marian dogmas (Immaculate Conception, Assumption) held as required belief | Honored as faith exemplar, without later dogmas |
| Saints | May be asked to intercede (dulia, not latria) | Christ alone mediates |
| Purgatory | Yes — temporary post-death purification | No — immediate presence with Christ |
| Prayer for the dead | Yes, including candle-lighting | Generally no |
