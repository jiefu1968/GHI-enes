# Cultural Clusters: Grouping the World's Cultures

Rather than treating every country as culturally unique, researchers (notably Livermore, building on earlier cluster studies) group countries that share similar historical, linguistic, religious, and value patterns into roughly ten clusters. Clusters are a starting hypothesis to test in a real encounter, never a substitute for knowing the specific person or people group in front of you.

1. **Anglo** — US, UK, Canada, Australia, NZ, Ireland — individualist, moderate power distance, direct communication.
2. **Nordic European** — Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland — low power distance, high individualism, egalitarian.
3. **Germanic European** — Germany, Austria, Switzerland — high uncertainty avoidance, structured, direct.
4. **Eastern European** — Russia, Poland, Hungary, etc. — higher power distance, relationship-based trust, historically shaped by both hierarchy and improvisation under scarcity.
5. **Latin European** — France, Italy, Spain, Portugal — moderate-high power distance, expressive/emotional communication, relationship-based.
6. **Latin American** — Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, etc. — high collectivism, high power distance, relationship-based trust, expressive.
7. **Confucian Asian** — China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, Korea, Singapore — high collectivism, high power distance, high-context communication, face-consciousness, long-term orientation.
8. **South Asian** — India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka — high power distance, collectivist but with strong individual/family achievement orientation, high-context.
9. **Sub-Saharan African** — widely diverse but often collectivist, relationship/oral-based, high respect for age and ancestry.
10. **Arab** — Middle East and North Africa — high power distance, collectivist, high-context, relationship-based trust, honor/shame social structure.

## Handling with care
Clusters describe modal tendencies across large populations and eras of research that are already dating in places (urbanization, migration, and generational change shift real behavior faster than cluster labels update). For missiological use, a cluster is a *hypothesis to test*, not a label to apply to an individual believer or a specific local congregation — always let direct relationship and observation (CQ Strategy and CQ Action, file 01) correct the cluster-level prediction.
