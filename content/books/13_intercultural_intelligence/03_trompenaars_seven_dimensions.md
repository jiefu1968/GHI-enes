# Trompenaars' Seven Dimensions of Culture

Fons Trompenaars (building on earlier work with Charles Hampden-Turner) framed culture as a set of dilemmas every society resolves differently. Seven dimensions are widely used in cross-cultural training:

1. **Universalism vs. Particularism** — apply the same rule to everyone regardless of relationship (universalist), or adapt the rule based on who is involved and the specific situation (particularist).

2. **Individualism vs. Communitarianism** — parallel to Hofstede's individualism/collectivism: is the individual or the group the primary unit of society?

3. **Neutral vs. Emotional** — is open emotional expression in professional/public settings normal (emotional) or seen as unprofessional and a loss of control (neutral)?

4. **Specific vs. Diffuse** — are relationships compartmentalized by domain — a colleague stays "just a colleague" (specific) — or do relationships spill across domains, so a business partner is also expected to be a personal friend (diffuse)?

5. **Achievement vs. Ascription** — is status earned through what you have accomplished (achievement), or is it granted by age, family, gender, or social connections (ascription)?

6. **Sequential vs. Synchronic Time** — is time a sequence of discrete, scheduled events done one at a time (sequential), or is it viewed as cyclical/overlapping, with multiple things happening in parallel and punctuality treated loosely (synchronic)?

7. **Internal vs. External Control** — do people believe they can shape and control their environment and outcomes (internal), or do they see themselves as needing to work in harmony with, or yield to, external forces — nature, fate, God's will (external)?

## Why this overlaps with, but isn't identical to, Hofstede
Trompenaars' dimensions are more relationally and situationally framed than Hofstede's population-level statistics; they are often more useful in live negotiation or team-conflict coaching because they describe *dilemmas in a specific interaction* rather than only national averages. The CFMulticultural assessment tool (file 06) uses items 21-48 to measure these seven dimensions.

## Missiological note
Dimension 7 (Internal vs. External Control) intersects directly with theology: a fatalistic "external control" worldview can either open a door to the gospel (dependence on a sovereign God) or, left unaddressed, slide into folk-religious appeasement of impersonal fate. Distinguishing biblical providence from fatalism is a key task of contextualized teaching in externally-oriented cultures.
