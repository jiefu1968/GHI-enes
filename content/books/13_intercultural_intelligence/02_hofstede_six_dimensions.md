# Hofstede's Six Dimensions of National Culture

Geert Hofstede's research, based on large-scale survey data across dozens of countries, identified recurring axes on which national cultures differ. These are population-level tendencies (averages), not predictions about any one individual.

1. **Power Distance** — the degree to which unequal distribution of power is expected and accepted. High-PD cultures expect visible hierarchy and deference to superiors; low-PD cultures expect flatter structures and question authority more readily.

2. **Individualism vs. Collectivism** — whether identity is drawn primarily from the self (individualist) or from group membership — family, team, company (collectivist). Most of the Chinese-speaking world and much of Asia, Africa, and Latin America sit toward the collectivist end; North America, Australia, and much of Northern Europe toward the individualist end.

3. **Masculinity vs. Femininity** — Hofstede's term for a society's emphasis on competition, achievement, and assertiveness ("masculine") versus cooperation, modesty, and quality of life ("feminine"). Best understood as a *toughness vs. tenderness* axis, not a statement about gender roles per se.

4. **Uncertainty Avoidance** — how threatened a culture feels by ambiguity and how much it relies on rules, structure, and formality to reduce it. High-UA cultures prefer detailed plans and written rules; low-UA cultures tolerate improvisation.

5. **Long-Term vs. Short-Term Orientation** — whether a culture emphasizes perseverance, thrift, and long-range planning (long-term) or quick results, tradition, and immediate reciprocity (short-term).

6. **Indulgence vs. Restraint** — the degree to which a society allows relatively free gratification of desires versus suppressing them through strict social norms.

## Cross-cultural ministry application
A missionary from a low-power-distance, individualist sending culture serving among a high-power-distance, collectivist host culture will likely misread deference to elders as passivity, and misread group decision-making as slowness — unless CQ Knowledge and CQ Strategy (see file 01) are actively engaged. The CFMulticultural assessment tool (file 06 in this module) maps its first 20 questions directly onto these six dimensions as a practical self-diagnostic.
