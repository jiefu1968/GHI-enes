# The CFMulticultural Self-Assessment Instrument

CFMulticultural has developed an 80-item, forced-choice (1-5 Likert scale) self-assessment that gives a practical, low-cost way for a missionary, team, or trainee to locate their own default cultural tendencies before entering — or while serving in — a cross-cultural assignment. It operationalizes three of the major academic frameworks covered in this module into a single instrument.

## Structure
| Section | Items | Dimensions covered |
|---|---|---|
| Hofstede | 1-20 | Power Distance (1-4), Masculinity/Toughness (5-8), Uncertainty Avoidance (9-12), Long-Term Orientation (13-16), Indulgence (17-20) |
| Trompenaars | 21-48 | Universalism (21-24), Specific/Diffuse (25-28), Neutral/Emotional (24-32, overlapping band), Achievement/Ascription (33-36), Sequential/Synchronic Time (37-40), Internal/External Control (41-44), Time Orientation (45-48) |
| Culture Map | 49-80 | High/Low Context (49-52), Evaluating (53-56), Persuading (57-60), Leading (61-64), Deciding (65-68), Trusting (69-72), Disagreeing (73-76), Scheduling (77-80) |

Each item is a paired statement; the respondent rates agreement 1 (Discordo/Disagree) to 5 (Concordo/Agree), with 3 as neutral. Scores within each 4-item band are averaged to produce a position on that specific dimension.

## How to use results pastorally and practically
- **Self-awareness first**: most people have never had their own defaults named. Simply seeing "I score high on Direct Feedback and low on Relationship-based Trust" reframes team friction from "that person is difficult" to "we have a predictable, nameable gap."
- **Pair with the field**: a candidate's CFMulticultural profile should be compared against the *host culture's* likely cluster profile (file 05) to predict where the biggest adaptation work will be needed — not to disqualify anyone, but to target pre-field training.
- **Re-administer over time**: CQ (file 01) is trainable; the same instrument taken again after 12-24 months on the field is a concrete way to show growth in CQ Knowledge and CQ Action, not just self-report of "I've adapted."
- **Team use**: administered across an entire team, it surfaces where the team's *internal* cross-cultural gaps are (e.g., team members from different sending countries) which are often larger and less anticipated than the gap with the host culture.

## Caution
Like Hofstede, Trompenaars, and Meyer's own instruments, this is a self-report tool measuring stated preference, not a personality diagnosis and not a predictor of ministry effectiveness by itself. It is one input into a larger discernment process, best interpreted with a mentor, coach, or member-care professional rather than in isolation.
