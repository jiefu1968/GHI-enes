# The Culture Map (Erin Meyer): Eight Scales for Working Across Cultures

Erin Meyer's framework focuses less on national trait-lists and more on *relative positioning*: every culture sits somewhere on each scale, and what matters most for a team or a missionary is the *gap* between two cultures on a given scale, not either culture's absolute position.

1. **Communicating** — Low-context (explicit, simple, literal messages that don't rely on shared background) vs. High-context (layered, nuanced, implicit messages that require reading between the lines).

2. **Evaluating** — Direct negative feedback (blunt, delivered publicly if needed) vs. Indirect negative feedback (softened, delivered privately, wrapped in positive framing).

3. **Persuading** — Principles-first (build the theoretical case, then move to application) vs. Applications-first (lead with a concrete example or recommendation, theory only if asked).

4. **Leading** — Egalitarian (the boss is one of the group; flat structure) vs. Hierarchical (the boss's status is marked and deferred to).

5. **Deciding** — Consensual (decisions built through group agreement) vs. Top-down (the leader decides, often after consultation).

6. **Trusting** — Task-based trust (built through reliable delivery on work) vs. Relationship-based trust (built through shared meals, time, and personal disclosure — trust the person, then trust the work).

7. **Disagreeing** — Confrontational (open disagreement is seen as healthy and separate from the relationship) vs. Avoids-confrontation (open disagreement threatens the relationship and is minimized).

8. **Scheduling** — Linear-time (one thing at a time, schedules are firm) vs. Flexible-time (multiple things at once, schedules adjust to relationships and circumstances).

## Why "gap," not "position," is the key idea
Two high-context cultures communicate fine with each other even though outsiders find both hard to read. The friction shows up when a low-context, direct-feedback, task-trust culture partners with a high-context, indirect-feedback, relationship-trust culture — the *size of the gap* on each scale predicts where misunderstanding will occur, more than either culture's individual position does.

## Ministry team application
A common cross-team conflict on multicultural mission teams: a Trusting-scale mismatch (task-based leader assumes relationship-based teammates are "just being friendly" rather than building the actual foundation of collaboration) compounded by a Disagreeing-scale mismatch (the task-based leader raises concerns directly in a team meeting; the relationship-based teammate experiences this as a relational rupture, not simply "feedback"). Diagnosing which of the eight scales is actually in play — rather than reaching for "it's a cultural thing" generically — is what CQ Strategy (file 01) looks like in practice.
