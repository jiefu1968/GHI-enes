# Cultural Intelligence (CQ): The Four-Factor Model (Livermore)

Cultural Intelligence (CQ) is the capability to function effectively across national, ethnic, and organizational cultures. David Livermore's model breaks CQ into four interrelated capabilities. All four are needed together — strength in one does not compensate for weakness in another.

## 1. CQ Drive (Motivation)
Your interest, confidence, and persistence when facing cultural difference. Someone with low CQ Drive avoids cross-cultural interaction or gives up quickly when it gets uncomfortable. For missionaries, CQ Drive is fed by a theological conviction (every people group matters to God, Rev 7:9) as much as by personality.

## 2. CQ Knowledge (Cognition)
Your understanding of how cultures are similar and different — including business/organizational systems, language, religious beliefs, and value dimensions (see Hofstede and Trompenaars files in this module). Knowledge without the other three factors produces an "armchair expert" who can describe a culture but not actually engage it well.

## 3. CQ Strategy (Metacognition)
Your ability to plan for, monitor, and adjust your understanding *during* a cross-cultural encounter. This is the reflective factor: noticing when your assumptions are wrong, checking your mental model against what is actually happening, and adjusting in real time.

## 4. CQ Action (Behavior)
Your ability to actually flex verbal and nonverbal behavior appropriately — pace, directness, formality, use of silence, gesture — when the situation calls for it. This is where CQ becomes visible to others.

## Why the sequence matters
Drive → Knowledge → Strategy → Action is not strictly linear, but it is diagnostic: a cross-cultural conflict can usually be traced to a breakdown in one specific factor. A team member who "knows the theory" (Knowledge) but keeps offending people (Action) has a strategy gap, not a knowledge gap — teach them to monitor and adjust in the moment rather than giving them more facts.

## Missiological application
Paul models all four factors in 1 Corinthians 9:19-23 ("I have become all things to all people") — high Drive (motivated by the gospel, not comfort), deep Knowledge of both Jewish and Gentile contexts, clear Strategy (he names his approach explicitly), and visible Action (circumcising Timothy in one context, refusing to in another, Gal 2:3 vs Acts 16:3, based on the situation's stakes). Cultural adaptation of this kind is wisdom, not compromise — the line to watch is theological content versus cultural form (see the Contextualization module, #23).
