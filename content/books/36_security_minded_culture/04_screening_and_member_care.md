# Screening and Member Care for Hostile-Context Service

## Overview

Synthesized from Hamilton T. Burke, Ph.D.'s "Serving in Hostile Places,
Volume Two," this framework is written primarily for sending
organizations (SOs), mentors, and member care providers (MCPs) rather
than for the international worker (IW) directly -- it addresses how an
organization prepares, screens, and supports people going into
persecuted or highly stressful ("+P") contexts. The book's core
premise, drawn from military combat psychiatry: everyone who serves
long enough in a high-stress "combat" environment will eventually
become a stress casualty in some form -- it is a matter of when, not
if -- and the goal of a support system is to prevent breakdowns where
possible and facilitate recovery when they happen, exactly as combat
psychiatry does for soldiers.

## Fear of Failure as the Core Threat

The heaviest psychological burden in hostile-context service is
usually not the danger itself but the fear of failure -- the
self-blame that follows when something goes wrong ("if only I'd been
stronger / seen it coming"). Left unaddressed, this self-blame is what
turns a normal stress reaction into a long-term casualty. The primary
goal of a support program should be to create an environment where the
IW can serve without fear of failure, which in turn produces more
confidence, effectiveness, and resilience in the field.

## Three Goals of a Support Program

1. **Unequivocal support** -- the MCP's role toward the IW should be
   purely supportive, not evaluative or growth-oriented. This rests on
   being able to say, truthfully, that an IW's struggles come from
   the stress of the work itself, not from an underlying, unaddressed
   condition -- which is only possible if proper screening (see below)
   has already ruled out other causes.
2. **Trust between MCP and IW** -- the IW needs to experience the MCP
   as a genuinely safe person, where confessing a struggle, fear, or
   failure will not be used against them or recorded in a way that
   threatens their future service. Without this trust, IWs process
   their hardest struggles alone, cut off from the support system that
   exists precisely to help them.
3. **Trust between MCP and the sending organization** -- the SO needs
   confidence that real issues are being identified and addressed
   (not hidden), while the IW and MCP need confidence that honest
   disclosure will not be weaponized administratively. Appropriate
   screening at the front end is part of what makes this three-way
   trust possible.

## Three Core Screening Areas (Before Deployment)

Recommended screening focuses on ruling out pre-existing conditions
that would complicate later "unequivocal support," rather than simply
checking a box:

- **Mental health conditions** -- diagnosable conditions, treatment
  history, unresolved trauma history, PTSD symptoms, and anxiety or
  fears that are intense but don't meet a full diagnostic threshold.
- **Personality assessment** -- personality disorders or strong
  personality traits (particularly Borderline or Narcissistic
  patterns), and specifically how that personality is likely to
  express itself under the amplified stress of a hostile environment
  (a trait that is manageable at home may become destabilizing under
  chronic threat).
- **"Fleshly" vulnerabilities** -- self-injurious behaviors, substance
  abuse and addictions, and patterns like pornography use -- areas
  that stress reliably intensifies, and that can quietly undermine a
  field team if not honestly assessed beforehand.

Additional useful areas: physical health conditions that could be
complicated by field conditions, and a candid look at **motivation**
for overseas service -- since motivations rooted in escaping personal
problems rather than a clear call tend to fare worse under sustained
stress.

## Application Focus

For mentors and sending-organization staff: building pre-field
screening conversations that are thorough without being invasive or
punitive; designing support relationships the IW will actually trust
enough to use honestly; and recognizing that struggle under extreme
stress is not, by itself, evidence of weakness, unfitness, or failure
-- distinguishing it from the separate, legitimate question of whether
a pre-existing condition needs to be addressed before or during
deployment.
