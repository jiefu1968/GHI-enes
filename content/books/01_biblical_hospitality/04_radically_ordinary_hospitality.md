# Radically Ordinary Hospitality: Everyday Practice (based on Rosaria Butterfield's framework)

## Overview

Where more academic treatments of hospitality focus on biblical
theology, this framework is written as a practical, personal account of
what sustained, daily hospitality actually costs and produces in an
increasingly post-Christian social environment. Its central claim is
that hospitality is not a program or an event but a lifestyle
orientation — described as "radically ordinary" because it happens
through routine, repeated, unglamorous acts of opening one's home,
rather than through occasional impressive gestures.

## Hospitality as Spiritual Warfare and Spiritual Discipline

A recurring theme is that genuine hospitality toward people with very
different beliefs and lifestyles is spiritually costly — it involves
real vulnerability, the risk of misunderstanding, and sustained
patience. Because of this cost, hospitality is framed not merely as a
social skill to acquire but as a spiritual discipline requiring
ongoing prayer and preparation, similar to fasting or Scripture
reading. The implicit warning for training purposes: missionaries who
expect hospitality to be easy or immediately rewarding will burn out;
those who understand it as a discipline practiced over years are more
likely to sustain it.

## Practical Postures Toward Neighbors of Different Beliefs

Several concrete relational postures are emphasized for engaging
neighbors who hold very different worldviews or lifestyles:
respecting the real complexity and dignity of neighbors' lives rather
than reducing them to a "project"; praying to become a safe person
neighbors feel able to confide in; carefully distinguishing between
holiness (conformity to God's character) and mere social respectability
so that the genuine goodness of unbelieving neighbors can be honestly
acknowledged rather than dismissed; refusing to assume ill intent in
those who hold different theological convictions; and grounding respect
for every neighbor in their being made in the image of God, regardless
of what they believe or how they live. The final practical instruction
is simply to begin — with whatever small act of welcome is possible
today, rather than waiting for ideal circumstances.

## God's Providence in Ordinary Encounters

A repeated narrative device is looking back over a lifetime of
seemingly random encounters and meals and recognizing, in retrospect,
a pattern of providence — that God was orchestrating relationships
through ordinary hospitality long before their significance was
visible. This reframes the missionary's daily, often unremarkable acts
of welcome (a shared meal, an open door, a repeated invitation) as
potentially significant in ways that may not be evident until much
later, encouraging perseverance through seasons when hospitality seems
to be producing no visible fruit.

## Application to cross-cultural and diaspora ministry

For missionaries entering communities with sharply different moral or
religious frameworks (secular, Muslim, Buddhist, or otherwise), the
practical postures here — safety, honest acknowledgment of genuine
goodness in the other, patience without assuming ill will, dignity
grounded in the imago Dei — offer a concrete, teachable pattern for
building trust across deep worldview difference without either
compromising conviction or communicating contempt.

## Discussion prompts for training

- What would it look like, concretely, to become "a safe person" for a
  specific neighbor or contact this month?
- Where might you be confusing holiness with mere social respectability
  in how you judge people from a very different background?
- Can you recall an ordinary act of hospitality whose significance only
  became clear much later? What does that suggest about persevering
  through seasons with no visible fruit?
