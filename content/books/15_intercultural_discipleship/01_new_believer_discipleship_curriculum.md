# A Nineteen-Lesson New-Believer Discipleship Curriculum: Structure and Themes

## Overview

This framework is a practical, lesson-by-lesson discipleship course
designed for new believers, moving from foundational trust in Christ
through spiritual disciplines, identity in Christ, and finally toward
becoming a reproducing, globally-minded disciple. Each lesson follows a
consistent format — a stated overview goal, specific learning
objectives, preparation notes for the leader, guided Bible study
questions, and practical action points — making it a template a new
believer could eventually lead for someone else, not just receive
passively.

## Lessons 1-3: Foundational Trust

The course opens by establishing sound, biblically grounded faith in
Christ as trustworthy (rather than blind or naive belief), then moves
to the new believer's changed relationship with God — forgiveness,
reconciliation, and permanence of that relationship — before
introducing the Holy Spirit's empowering presence in daily living.
Building trust and identity before behavior modification reflects a
deliberate pedagogical sequence: a new believer needs settled assurance
of relationship with God before the course asks anything of their
conduct.

## Lessons 6-10: Outward Life and Identity

Later lessons address sharing the gospel with others, deepening the
believer's relationship with God the Father specifically, understanding
new life in Christ, standing firm using the "armor of God" image from
Ephesians, and the ministry of reconciliation drawn from 2 Corinthians.
This section balances inward relationship (with the Father) and outward
mission (sharing faith, offering reconciliation to others), treating
both as inseparable dimensions of a healthy new believer's life.

## Lessons 11-14: Confidence, Obedience, and Multiplication

A notable lesson on "expanding your circle of confidence" uses David's
confrontation with Goliath (1 Samuel 17) to teach that confidence
in God grows through remembering his past provision and reflecting on
his specific attributes — a concrete, reproducible method for building
faith rather than simply exhorting a new believer to "trust more."
Subsequent lessons address obedience as the natural fruit of a trusting
relationship with God, a specific biblical strategy for spiritual
multiplication (equipping believers not just to grow personally but to
reproduce disciples themselves), and identification with Christ
(understanding one's new identity as united with Christ's death and
resurrection).

## Lessons 15-17: Spiritual Disciplines and Guidance

The course addresses prayer, temptation, and discerning God's will as
practical disciplines every new believer needs concrete tools for,
rather than assuming these skills develop automatically. Framing
prayer, resisting temptation, and discovering God's will as learnable,
teachable skills (with specific guided questions and action points
rather than only inspirational encouragement) reflects the course's
consistent pedagogical commitment to concrete reproducibility.

## Lessons 18-19: Global Vision and Life Purpose

The closing lessons deliberately widen the new believer's horizon
beyond personal growth alone — "becoming a world Christian" introduces
global gospel need and the believer's potential role in it, and "making
my life count" challenges the believer to consider their whole life's
direction and purpose in light of Christ's call. Placing global
missionary vision near the very end of a foundational discipleship
course (rather than treating it as an advanced, optional topic) signals
that world Christian vision is considered part of basic discipleship,
not a specialization reserved for a few.

## Pedagogical Method

Across all nineteen lessons, the format remains deliberately consistent:
a stated goal and specific learning objectives orient the leader before
teaching; preparation notes assume the leader has personally worked
through the material rather than merely presenting it; guided
Bible-study questions (often closed or limited in form for very new
believers) build confidence with the biblical text itself; and closing
"action points" require concrete behavioral response rather than
ending at intellectual comprehension. This consistent structure is
itself a discipleship-multiplication tool: a growing believer who has
been through the course as a learner already has a workable template
for leading someone else through the same nineteen lessons.

## Application to cross-cultural and diaspora ministry

The curriculum explicitly supplements its lessons with recommended
Chinese-language resources for further reading, indicating it was
developed with a Chinese-speaking discipleship context specifically in
mind. Its closed-question format for early lessons (an adaptation for
very new believers unfamiliar with independent Bible study) offers a
transferable model for any oral or low-literacy context where fully
open-ended Bible study questions may initially overwhelm rather than
build confidence.

## Discussion prompts for training

- How does building assurance of relationship with God (lessons 1-3)
  before addressing behavior reflect a deliberate pedagogical choice,
  and where might that sequence apply in your own discipleship
  practice?
- The Goliath lesson (11) teaches confidence-building through
  remembering God's past provision — what past provision would you use
  to build a specific new believer's confidence in your own context?
- Why might placing "becoming a world Christian" near the end of a
  foundational course (rather than as an advanced specialty) matter for
  how new believers understand their own missionary potential from the
  very start of their faith?
