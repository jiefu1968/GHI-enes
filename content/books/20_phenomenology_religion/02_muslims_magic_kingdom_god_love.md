# Muslims, Magic and the Kingdom of God: Church Planting Among Folk Muslims (based on Rick Love's framework)

## Overview

This framework addresses ministry among "folk Muslims" — the
substantial majority of the world's Muslims whose lived religious
practice includes significant folk elements (amulets, healers,
protective ritual, spirit belief) alongside formal Islamic
theology, distinguishing them from the more theologically formal,
official Islam typically presented in comparative religion textbooks.
As missiologist J. Dudley Woodberry's observation (cited as this
framework's touchstone) puts it, the felt need for power is so central
among folk Muslims that their entire worldview is seen through the lens
of power — meaning effective ministry must directly address power and
protection, not merely doctrinal argument.

## Showman or Shaman? Understanding the Folk Muslim Worldview

Folk Muslims often perceive spiritual power as the decisive category
determining whether any religious claim, Christian or Islamic, is
credible — a miracle worker or healer commands attention and respect
in a way that abstract theological argument does not, meaning
missionaries need to understand whether they are being perceived as a
mere "showman" (an entertaining but powerless outsider) or as someone
who might possess genuine spiritual authority a "shaman" or traditional
power figure would also be expected to have. This worldview orientation
shapes evangelistic strategy directly: presenting the gospel primarily
as an intellectual or doctrinal system, without addressing questions of
spiritual power and protection, may fail to engage folk Muslims at the
level where their actual religious concerns operate.

## Contextualization

Contextualizing the gospel for folk Muslim audiences requires careful,
ongoing attention to how the biblical text actually relates to a folk
Muslim's specific cultural and religious context — neither imposing a
foreign cultural package alongside the gospel's essential content, nor
allowing genuine biblical truth to be diluted or distorted in an
attempt to seem more culturally familiar. This includes careful
discernment about which folk Islamic practices and assumptions can be
redirected toward Christ, and which must be directly confronted as
incompatible with biblical faith.

## Truth Encounter: A Liberating Lord

Alongside power encounter, the framework insists on sustained "truth
encounter" — directly teaching what Scripture reveals about Christ's
lordship and identity — since power alone, without clear biblical
truth content, risks producing converts who simply add Christ as
another source of spiritual power within an unchanged folk worldview,
rather than genuinely submitting to Christ's unique lordship. Specific
biblical "power passages" are examined for what they teach about
Christ's authority over spiritual forces, providing direct scriptural
content for engaging a worldview already primed to take spiritual
power seriously.

## Power Encounter: Offensive Attack

Because folk Muslims already operate within a worldview that takes
spiritual power, protection, and confrontation seriously, the
framework argues that avoiding any direct engagement with power
questions cedes important spiritual and evangelistic ground.
"Power encounter" — direct, prayerful confrontation with spiritual
forces folk Muslims already believe are real and active, demonstrating
Christ's superior power and authority — is presented as a necessary,
biblically grounded evangelistic strategy for this specific audience,
rather than an optional supplement to verbal gospel presentation.

## Application to cross-cultural and missionary training

This framework directly equips missionaries serving Muslim populations
with substantial folk religious practice (common across much of
Central Asia, parts of the Middle East, North Africa, and South and
Southeast Asia) to move beyond purely doctrinal apologetic engagement
and address the power and protection concerns that actually shape folk
Muslim worldview and daily life. Combining truth encounter (clear
biblical teaching about Christ's identity and lordship) with power
encounter (direct engagement with spiritual power questions) offers a
more complete strategy than either alone.

## Discussion prompts for training

- How would you distinguish being perceived as a "shaman" possessing
  genuine spiritual authority from being dismissed as a mere
  "showman," in your specific ministry context?
- Why does this framework insist that power encounter and truth
  encounter must go together, rather than relying on either alone?
- What folk Islamic practices in your context might be genuinely
  redirected toward Christ, and which would need direct, respectful
  confrontation as incompatible with biblical faith?
